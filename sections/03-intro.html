Part Two

INTRODUCTION

Like the natural systems I studied in my MFA thesis work (2009–2011), my work has followed a branching path across teaching, creative practice, professional practice, and research. The strands sometimes intertwine and sometimes diverge, but one commitment has remained constant. I am interested in pushing visual communication design past its usual boundaries, using multisensory methods and new technologies to make our world more human and accessible. Since my promotion to Associate Professor in 2021, that commitment has shaped my work at Jefferson, and this dossier documents how it has grown into sustained, externally recognized achievement in teaching, scholarship, practice, and service.

I cannot reflect on this period without acknowledging the pandemic. Teaching through pixels on screens and masks on faces stripped away much of what makes studio learning human: the facial expressions that convey comfort and ease, and the tactile experience unique to the studio. Papers, printers, and pin-up walls all became pixels. That experience pushed me to ask what makes an interaction feel human, and how communication can reach people through more than sight alone. This change marked a turning point in my research, a shift toward designing more human interactions with technology.

Teaching. Teaching remains the center of my appointment. My information design and experience design courses turned toward the pandemic itself, with students producing award-winning work on safe signage in public transit and an information graphics publication on the pandemic’s effects on culture and society. My mentoring of Health Communication Design graduate students grew alongside partnerships with harm reduction specialists and the Germantown CDC. Projects like these are where I see learning happen at a level that connects students to real people and real issues. I developed Creative Mapping, an elective exploring geographic and non-geographic mapping through multisensory and socio-spatial methods, and continued developing the MS in Health Communication Design capstone sequence. A Nexus Learning grant collaboration with Interior Design faculty earned a Dean’s Award for Collaboration, and The Blurring Boundaries, which I co-led, received the Provost Team Award for Interdisciplinary Education. Through the Erasmus+ Mobility Program, I co-taught an intensive module at IUAV University of Venice with Jefferson architecture colleagues, bringing my sensory mapping methods into an international studio.

Research and creative work. In 2022, I was named a “Responsible Design to Watch” by GDUSA, a national recognition aligned with the partnerships I had been building in health communication. A 2023 SEED grant then allowed me to launch Project Data Sense, an ongoing laboratory for multisensory data translation. It produced Seeing Bird Songs, published in the print edition of Nightingale Magazine’s Nature issue. Its methods extended to the body in Energy Bodies, selected for the internationally juried Information+ 2025 at the MIT Media Lab, and into public space through Intersections: Health, Environment & Design, presented alongside the Venice Architecture Biennale and reinstalled for DesignPhiladelphia. My collaboration with Dr. Greg Jaffe (SKMC) applying design to harm reduction and stigma reduction has produced a co-authored paper now accepted for publication. The work now has competitive funding for its next phase through an AACSRE Emerging Fellowship for 2026–2027.

Professional practice. For fifteen years, my studio, Gold Collective, has kept my research connected to real communication needs. With Dr. Daniel Morgan of the University of Maryland, I designed an NIH-funded toolkit for teaching clinical testing, including a decision map, testing calculators, a game, and animations that help practitioners judge the likelihood of a true or false result. Other recent work includes an identity and wayfinding system for an exhibition at the deCordova Sculpture Park and Museum and a comprehensive rebrand for a communications firm.

Service and leadership. As co-chair of AIGA Philadelphia’s Education Committee, I worked with co-chair Mary Katie Leech to transform a 25-year-running regional portfolio review into a virtual conference, preserving a key professional milestone for students across the region. This process was presented at an AIGA National Board Meeting as a method for other chapters across the US to replicate. At Jefferson, I chair the Shared Governance Committee and serve on the Kanbar Executive Committee and the Kanbar Diversity Committee. As Coordinator of the DEC Capstone Experience, I manage capstone programming across Design, Engineering, and Commerce, connecting students to each other, to research resources, and to alumni networks at the point in their education when those connections matter most. Most recently, I was appointed Director of the Jefferson Center for Immersive Arts and Health, which brings Jefferson’s design disciplines together around research that puts human health at the center of how we design products, spaces, and communications.

Five years ago, I was learning how to teach through a screen. Today I am asking how design can make the technology we depend on feel human again, a question I have explored through my Project Data Sense research and carried into my classroom. I intend to keep working toward this goal as I extend my work across Jefferson and build on the Jefferson Center for Immersive Arts and Health.
