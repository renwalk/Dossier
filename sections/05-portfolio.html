Project Data Sense

Funded — SEED Grant 2023–2024

Project Data Sense

Project Data Sense began with experiments translating weather data into color, using perceived color properties to make conditions something that could be felt rather than only read. The work has grown into a body of research spanning temperature dashboards, bird songs, body-movement visualization, and more.

Supported by a 2023 SEED grant, I worked with two research assistants to move Project Data Sense from concept to a body of working prototypes spanning haptic wearables, tactile cues, environmental graphics, and learning tools — developed through direct community engagement, including testing sessions with regional birding groups and accessibility advocates.

exhibited 2023

Temperature Quilt

As temperatures warm around the globe, the Current Temp Quilt visualizes temperatures from different cities around the globe with jSON weather data. Like the feeling of heat on your skin, this display converts current temperature data to color values on a scale from cold to warm mapped with cooler temps in the blue-green range to hotter in the red-orange. The display allows for comparison among cities giving you an at a glance sense of what the world feels like right now.

  1. Temperature quiltNine cities, each a square of color: the square shows the actual temperature and the circle inside it shows how the temperature feels, on a scale from 0 (teal) to 100 (orange).

Seeing Bird Songs

Seeing Bird Songs uses algorithmic methods to translate bird vocalizations into visual forms that make each species recognizable through multiple sensory channels — developed through direct testing with regional birding groups and accessibility advocates, and published in the print edition of Nightingale Magazine’s Nature issue.

Energy Bodies

Developed with expressive arts therapist Carrie Powell, Energy Bodies is an interactive motion-capture installation that translates emotion, movement quality, spatial orientation, and body shape into a computational visual system grounded in somatic design theory. The work was selected for Information+ 2025 at the MIT Media Lab, on view from November 2025 through January 2026.

  1. Installation viewEnergy Bodies at Information+ 2025, MIT Media Lab: a portrait display with a camera for body tracking, an iPad stand for adjusting the visualization, and the project panel with printed session snapshots.

Moonflower

Moonflower is a kaleidoscope that takes its form from the Moon. The current phase, distance, and angle, calculated for the viewer’s location, shape the pattern, which can be adjusted with sliders or set in motion. I am currently writing an article about this project for the next issue of Nightingale Magazine.

Gold Collective

Gold Collective is the visual communication design studio I founded and lead as Founding Partner and Creative Director. The studio specializes in identity systems, exhibition design, printed matter, and visual communication for social impact, working with institutions, nonprofits, and cultural partners to develop storytelling strategies that translate complex data and systems into accessible, emotionally resonant experiences.

For fifteen years the studio has kept my research connected to real communication needs. The projects in this section were made through Gold Collective.

Land Portal

Through Gold Collective, I designed the printed walking tour guide for Land Portal, a walk through ecological space and time by Ellie Irons and Aubrie James. This was a land ecology piece at deCordova Sculpture Park and Museum, adjacent to the Nature Sanctuary exhibition. I worked with the artists and museum curators to create a piece that felt reminiscent of ephemera from the historical archives in the museum by sticking to a two-color format and print process. Typography highlights language and knowledge from the Aquinnah Wampanoag nation that once inhabited the land.

  1. The guide laid flat, front and back.

  1. The map in useThe route map, opened on the grounds at deCordova.

Testing Wisely

Led by Dr. Daniel Morgan of the University of Maryland, Testing Wisely is an NIH-funded initiative to improve clinical testing decisions. I put together a team of animators, illustrators, and web developers and led the creative direction of a suite of teaching animations, an educational game, and a disease-risk calculator, along with a decision map that helps practitioners judge the likelihood of a true or false result.

  1. Testing Wisely websiteThe home page, organizing the project’s tools: teaching videos, a diagnosis calculator, a test playground, and a game. testingwisely.com ↗
  1. Disease risk calculatorThe calculator, set to HIV: pre-test probability, test choice, and a grid showing what happens when 100 patients are tested.
  2. Disease risk playgroundSliders for pre-test probability, sensitivity, and specificity update the same 100-patient grid.
  1. Decision map: troponinA one-page “Should I test?” map for troponin and heart attack: whether testing makes a difference, the chance before testing, what a positive result means, and benefits against harms.
  1. Decision map: HIVThe same map, built for an HIV test.
