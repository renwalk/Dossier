Part Three

RESEARCH,SCHOLARSHIP,AND/ORCREATIVEWORK

My research practice uses data, perceptual principles, and sensory information to make communication design more accessible. Within this practice, I pursue several related questions: how communication can reach people through more than one sense, how perceptual principles can inform clearer and more inclusive design, and how data that is invisible or difficult to feel, whether ecological, somatic, or environmental, can become something people perceive and understand. Since my promotion to Associate Professor in 2021, this practice has grown from exploratory work in creative coding into a sustained research program that has reached national and international venues, earned juried exhibition and selective publication, and secured competitive fellowship funding to carry the work forward.

I.

Establishing a Research Program: Project Data Sense (2021–2023)

Project Data Sense is an ongoing initiative exploring the multisensory translation of environmental and ecological data. It began with experiments translating weather data into color, using perceived color properties to make conditions something that could be felt rather than only read. That work led to its first major outcome, Seeing Bird Songs, which uses algorithmic methods to translate bird vocalizations into visual forms that make each species recognizable through multiple sensory channels.

Supported by a 2023 SEED grant, I worked with two research assistants to move Project Data Sense from concept to a body of working prototypes spanning haptic wearables, tactile cues, environmental graphics, and learning tools. The project was developed through direct community engagement, including testing sessions with regional birding groups and accessibility advocates. This established the working method I have used since, grounding technical experimentation in lived and inclusive use. My article on the project was selected as a feature in the print edition of Nightingale Magazine’s Nature issue, a respected publication in the data visualization field.

The technical foundation for this work came from advanced study in data visualization (2021) and in p5.js and DrawBot at Cooper Union (2022). I also joined a community of birders, artists, designers, and scientists in Binoculars to Binomials, a course created by data artist Jer Thorp, former data artist in residence at The New York Times. This period marked a deliberate shift in my research identity from client-driven professional practice toward a self-directed creative research agenda, and it set the direction for the work that follows.

II.

Multisensory Systems and International Recognition (2025–2026)

In summer 2025, I extended the methods of Project Data Sense to the body in Energy Bodies, an interactive motion-capture installation developed with expressive arts therapist Carrie Powell. Energy Bodies translates emotion, movement quality, spatial orientation, and body shape into a computational visual system grounded in somatic design theory. The work was selected for Information+ 2025 at the MIT Media Lab, on view from November 2025 through January 2026. Information+ is a competitive, internationally juried venue for information design research, and the installation’s acceptance places my work within global conversations about embodied interfaces and data-driven creative practice.

That same year, I contributed to Intersections: Health, Environment & Design, presented at the Venice Architecture Biennale. In collaboration with architecture faculty and students, I moderated an international panel on environmental data and urban health and co-developed exhibition components that were installed in Venice and later reinstalled at the Center for Architecture and Design for DesignPhiladelphia 2025, carrying the project from an international stage back into the regional design community.

III.

Public Health and Transdisciplinary Design

My collaborations in public health apply the same commitment to accessible communication to health education, where clear and stigma-free design can shape who seeks information and care. With Dr. Greg Jaffe of Sidney Kimmel Medical College, I have applied design methods to harm reduction and stigma reduction. This work produced a co-authored paper, now accepted for publication in [journal], examining a designed harm-reduction intervention. The project also informed a 2025 submission to the Smart & Healthy Cities initiative with Nioka Wyatt of Fashion Merchandising. Together, these efforts position design as an active tool in public health education and behavioral change and reflect the cross-college, transdisciplinary collaboration the University increasingly values.

Research in the Classroom

This research directly shapes my teaching. V.

Ongoing Professional Practice

Throughout this period I have maintained an active professional practice through my studio, Gold Collective, keeping my research connected to real client needs. Notable work includes continued collaboration with the deCordova Sculpture Park and Museum, most recently exhibition graphics, wayfinding, and a walking tour guide for a land ecology piece adjacent to the exhibition. I also led the creative direction of a suite of teaching animations, an educational game, and a disease-risk calculator for Testing Wisely, an NIH-funded initiative led by Dr. Daniel Morgan of the University of Maryland to improve clinical testing decisions. This dual track of academic research and applied professional practice is characteristic of practice-based scholarship in visual communication design and reflects the balance that practice-faculty criteria are designed to recognize.

VII.

Looking Forward: The AACSRE Emerging Fellowship

This trajectory, from Seeing Bird Songs through Energy Bodies and Intersections, has led to my selection as an AACSRE Emerging Fellow for 2026–2027. The fellowship funds the development of responsive, multisensory smart signage systems that translate real-time ecological and environmental data into urban experience. It extends the sensory and perceptual methods at the center of this body of work into public space and continues a question I have pursued since 2021: how design can make the invisible perceptible. More broadly, it advances the aim that connects all of my research, communication design that more people can perceive, understand, and act on.

Conclusion

Taken together, the research, creative work, and professional practice documented here form a coherent practice that uses data, perceptual principles, and sensory information to make communication design more accessible, and they show clear and sustained achievement during my associate professorship: juried international exhibition, selective and peer-reviewed publication, national invited presentations, and competitively funded work that extends the program forward. Combined with continued professional practice and active contribution to the standards and shared knowledge of the design profession, this record reflects the national recognition and scholarly and professional achievement expected of a candidate for promotion to Professor.
