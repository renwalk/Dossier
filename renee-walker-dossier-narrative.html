PROMOTION DOSSIER — DRAFT · RESEARCH, SCHOLARSHIP & CREATIVE ACTIVITY

Renée Walker

Associate Professor of Visual Communication Design, Thomas Jefferson University

Since submitting my 2020 dossier, my research has developed around a single methodological question: how can design translate data that is otherwise invisible, inaccessible, or hard to feel — whether ecological, somatic, or environmental — into experiences people can perceive with more than their eyes. What began as a set of exploratory experiments in creative coding and typography has grown into a sustained research program that has reached national and international venues, generated peer-reviewed and juried publication, and, most recently, secured competitive fellowship funding to carry the work forward.

I.

Foundational Development: Establishing a Research Practice (2020–2023)

The early part of this period was one of deliberate methodological groundwork. In 2021, I completed an intensive typeface design workshop and began developing my own typeface across print and dimensional applications, work that sharpened my formal vocabulary for the sensory and material explorations that would follow. In 2022, coursework in p5.js at Cooper Union gave me the technical foundation to work directly with data in a coding environment built for designers, opening the door to the research direction I have pursued since.

That direction became Project Data Sense, an ongoing initiative exploring multisensory translation of environmental and ecological data. Its first major outcome, Seeing Bird Songs, uses algorithmic methods to translate bird vocalizations into visual form and was published in the print edition of Nightingale Magazine’s Nature issue, a respected publication in the data visualization field. The project was developed with direct community engagement, including testing sessions with regional birding groups and accessibility advocates — establishing a working method, grounding technical experimentation in lived, inclusive use, that has carried through every project since.

Supported by a SEED grant during this period, I used research assistantships to move Project Data Sense from concept to a body of working prototypes spanning haptic wearables, tactile cues, environmental graphics, and learning tools. This was the period in which my research identity shifted from client-driven professional practice toward a self-directed creative research agenda — a transition I was navigating without established institutional models to follow, but one that set the direction for everything that follows in this narrative.

II.

Multisensory Systems and International Recognition (2023–2025)

The methodology developed through Project Data Sense reached its most fully realized expression in Energy Bodies, an interactive motion-capture installation developed in collaboration with expressive arts therapist Carrie Powell. Energy Bodies translates emotion, movement quality, spatial orientation, and body shape into a computational visual system grounded in somatic design theory. In 2025, the work was selected for Information+ 2025 at the MIT Media Lab, on view from November 2025 through January 2026. Information+ is a competitive, internationally juried venue for information design research, and the installation’s acceptance situates my work directly within global conversations about embodied interfaces and data-driven creative practice.

That same year, I contributed to Intersections: Health, Environment & Design, presented alongside programming at the Venice Architecture Biennale. I moderated an international panel on environmental data and urban health and co-developed exhibition components that were installed in Venice and later reinstalled at the Center for Architecture and Design for DesignPhiladelphia 2025, extending the project’s reach from a global stage back into the regional design community. Complementing this work, I co-taught an intensive module at IUAV University of Venice through the Erasmus+ Mobility Program, broadening both the audience for my research and the cross-cultural range of my teaching practice.

III.

Public Health and Transdisciplinary Design

Alongside this visual and sensory research, I have built a sustained collaboration with Dr. Greg Jaffe of Sidney Kimmel Medical College applying design methods to harm reduction and stigma reduction. This work produced a co-authored paper, now accepted for publication, examining a harm-reduction educational intervention developed with students and installed in a fashion pop-up setting. The project also contributed to a 2025 submission to the Smart & Healthy Cities initiative with colleague Nioka Wyatt of Fashion Merchandising, positioning design as an active tool in public health education and behavioral change, and demonstrating the kind of cross-college, transdisciplinary collaboration this University increasingly values.

IV.

Ongoing Professional Practice

Throughout this period I have maintained an active professional design practice through Gold Collective, my design studio, keeping my creative research connected to real client needs. Notable work includes continued design collaboration with the deCordova Sculpture Park and Museum — most recently wayfinding and exhibition graphics for their Nature Sanctuaries exhibition and accompanying walking tour guide — and ongoing collaboration on Testing Wisely, an NIH-funded initiative led by Dr. Daniel Morgan focused on improving clinical testing decision-making, for which I have designed a suite of teaching animations, an educational game, and a disease-risk calculator. This dual track — academic research alongside applied professional practice — is characteristic of practice-based scholarship in visual communication design and reflects the balance the promotion criteria for practice faculty are built to recognize.

V.

Contributions to the Profession

My service to the discipline has been sustained and varied. I have served as a peer reviewer for the Journal of Communication in Healthcare, as a manuscript reviewer for Bloomsbury Publishing, and as a paper reviewer for the AIGA Design Educators Community conference. I have been invited to speak at national venues including the AIGA National Conference Design Educators Symposia and as keynote speaker for Method Madness at Phoenix Design Week, and I served as guest juror for the Revealed exhibition at the University of Delaware. Within the profession’s regional infrastructure, I served on the AIGA Philadelphia board as Education Chair, coordinating large-scale student portfolio reviews and educator programming for the Philadelphia design community.

VI.

Looking Forward: The AACSRE Emerging Fellowship

This trajectory — from early experiments in creative coding, through Seeing Bird Songs, to the fully realized systems of Energy Bodies and Intersections — has culminated in my selection as an AACSRE Emerging Fellow for 2026–2027. The fellowship funds continued development of responsive, multisensory smart signage systems that translate real-time ecological and environmental data into urban experience, a direct extension of the perceptual translation methodology at the center of this entire body of work.

Rather than a departure, the fellowship represents the next application of a research question I have been developing since 2021: how design can make the invisible perceptible, and in doing so, expand who gets to understand and act on the data shaping their environment.
VII.

Conclusion

Taken together, the research, creative work, and professional practice documented here represent a sustained and increasingly recognized trajectory — moving from foundational technical exploration through nationally and internationally exhibited creative research, peer-reviewed and juried publication, and now competitively funded forward-looking work. This body of work, combined with continued professional practice and active contribution to the design profession’s shared knowledge and standards, reflects the scholarly and professional achievement expected of a candidate for promotion to Professor.
